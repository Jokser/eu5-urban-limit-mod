[h1]Urban Limit Reworked[/h1]

In vanilla, any populous rural district can become a city, and by the late game whole countries are covered in half-empty towns. This mod keeps urbanization limited and believable: a city has to be a real urban place, a province holds only so many towns, and towns grow only where geography could support them.

[h2]What makes a city[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]City: 5,000 burghers[/b] living in the location, instead of 30,000 total population. Vanilla counted peasants and laborers too, so mining and farming districts became "cities" while real burgher towns with little countryside could not.
[*][b]Megalopolis: 25,000 burghers[/b], plus the vanilla conditions: a market center, the capital, an independent country. A rare rank for the great trade capitals.
[*][b]Town:[/b] the vanilla population requirement, plus the geography rules below.
[*]Existing towns and cities keep their rank. The rules only decide new upgrades.
[/list]

Each institution the owner has embraced adds its own bonus.

The AI's own hard limit (one settlement in any province of up to 5 locations) is relaxed, so the AI can actually use these caps. Upgrade goods costs are 2x vanilla.

[h2]Geography[/h2]
Each climate has its own rule for towns and for cities. Rivers, good harbours and lake shores let towns grow in harsh land. Farming resources feed market towns. Valuable trade goods (silk, saffron, cotton, gems, silver, gold, incense) build cities even in hard country. Deserts, tundra and bare mountains mostly stay rural. A [b]market center[/b] can always become a city outside the arctic: trade made places like Nuremberg great regardless of the site. The upgrade tooltip shows the rule for the location's climate.

[h2]Tested against history[/h2]
Observer runs from 1337 to 1800 were compared with Jan de Vries, [i]European Urbanization 1500-1800[/i]: the number of European cities with 10,000+ inhabitants.
[table]
[tr][th]Year[/th][th]1500[/th][th]1550[/th][th]1600[/th][th]1700[/th][th]1800[/th][/tr]
[tr][td]Cities in game (Europe)[/td][td]127[/td][td]186[/td][td]256[/td][td]434[/td][td]581[/td][/tr]
[tr][td]Cities 10k+ (de Vries)[/td][td]154[/td][td]~180[/td][td]220[/td][td]224[/td][td]364[/td][/tr]
[/table]
[list]
[*]Up to 1600 the game follows history closely. Italy and the German / Low Countries core match almost one to one.
[*]After 1650 the game keeps growing while real Europe stagnated (the 17th-century crisis), so the late game runs higher. The same happened with the old population rule. The gap narrows again in the 18th century.
[*]No "fake" cities: by 1800 no city has fewer than 3,000 burghers, and new towns founded by the AI average about 25,000 people, not empty villages.
[*]Megalopolises stay rare: by 1800 only Constantinople, Naples, Prague and Krakow in Europe.
[/list]
A perfect match is not possible with the game's economy and AI. The goal is a believable middle between no cities and every location urbanized.

[h2]Compatibility[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Do not use together with the original Urban limit.[/b] They share some triggers.
[*]Replaces [i]in_game/common/location_ranks/00_default.txt[/i] (generated from the current game version). Mods that change the same file will conflict.
[*]Save compatible: it can be added to a running game.
[/list]
